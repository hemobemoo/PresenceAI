## How to read the anchors

- Five dimensions follow the AAC&U VALUE Oral Communication rubric, each with four levels: 4 Capstone, 3 Milestone 3, 2 Milestone 2, 1 Benchmark. `followup_handling` is added with the same scale; it is not part of the VALUE rubric (FINAL_05 §1).
- Every anchor describes **observable behaviour with a number**. A level is reached when all of its computable conditions hold; the dimension gets the highest level reached; level 1 is the default.
- **A missing measure is missing, never low.** A measure that cannot be computed (camera off, no time limit, Arabic content, unreliable punctuation, no follow-up answer recorded) is dropped from the conditions. A dimension with no computable measure is reported as not measurable, never as level 1.
- Skill score for the adaptive engine: y = (level − 1) / 3, so 0, 0.33, 0.67, 1.
- **Every number is provisional** and is re-estimated after E5/E6. Numbers from the plan (FINAL_04) are the opening 20 %, the closing 15 % and the first 2 sentences.
- Introduction, conclusion, thesis and transition cues are **lexicon matches**, not an understanding of meaning or structure.
- "Left out" lists the parts of the VALUE wording that no rule can observe. They are not scored by rules. A language-model check may comment on some of them, only with consent (d), only with a quoted transcript span, never overriding a rule-based level, and never as a numeric level (ADR 0017).

## Definitions

- **opening**: The first 20 % of the words of the transcript.
- **closing**: The last 15 % of the words of the transcript.
- **body**: The words between the opening and the closing.
- **sentence**: A stretch of words ended by a sentence mark in the transcript. English sentence positions rest on Whisper's punctuation (ADR 0014); for Arabic they are not measurable (ADR 0011, ADR 0016).
- **thesis sentence**: The first sentence in the opening that matches the thesis-cue lexicon ("today I will", "my main point is", "I will show that"). A lexicon match, not an understanding of meaning.
- **key phrase**: The lemmatised head nouns of the noun phrases of the thesis sentence (spaCy en_core_web_sm; stop words left out). Matching is by lemma, so "result" and "results" match; it is not an exact-string match and not a match of meaning.
- **key-phrase recurrence**: A sentence of the body that contains at least one key phrase.
- **introduction cue**: A purpose or preview phrase in the opening ("in this talk", "I will cover"), from a lexicon.
- **conclusion cue**: A closing phrase in the closing ("in conclusion", "to sum up"), from a lexicon. Both cues are lexicon matches, not an understanding of the structure.
- **transition**: A sequence or transition cue between parts ("first", "next", "finally"), from a lexicon.
- **hook**: One of the first 2 sentences is a question, contains a statistic, opens a story ("when I was", "last year"), is a quotation, or makes a contrast claim ("most people think ... but"). Pattern rules; the false-positive rate is measured on C2 (P2-e).
- **mid-sentence pause**: A pause of at least 1 s between two words of the same sentence, by the transcript's punctuation (ADR 0011). English rests on Whisper's punctuation (ADR 0014); not measurable for Arabic (ADR 0016).
- **supporting-material types**: statistic (a number, percentage or quantity about the topic; list numbers, years used as dates and times of day are not counted); example ("for example", "for instance", "such as"); analogy ("like a", "as if", "imagine", "similar to"); quotation (words in quotation marks, or "said" / "according to" with a source). All are pattern rules.
- **counting supporting items**: One item per sentence and type: a sentence with two statistics counts as one statistic; a sentence with a statistic and an example counts as one of each. Items is the number of such (sentence, type) pairs; types is the number of distinct types.
- **missing measure**: A measure that cannot be computed (camera off, no time limit, Arabic content, unreliable punctuation, no follow-up answer recorded) is missing, never a low value: its conditions are dropped. A dimension with no computable measure is "not measurable", never level 1.
- **follow-up answer**: The speaker's answer to one follow-up question, recorded on its own. The recording starts automatically when the question ends (no manual start), so the answer's timeline begins at the end of the question (ADR 0022).
- **relevance**: Cosine similarity between the question and the answer transcript, from the knowledge-base embedding model (multilingual-e5-small, configs/models.yaml). The weaker answer decides: the lowest of the answers' similarities. A similarity of words, not an understanding of whether the question was answered; an evasive answer that reuses the question's words can score high. One on-topic cut calibrated on 20 question-answer pairs written by the owner (ADR 0022): it flags only answers that share little wording with the question.
- **response latency**: Seconds from the start of the answer recording (the end of the question) to the first speech onset found by voice activity detection. The weaker answer decides: the longest latency. If the question is shown as text, the reading time is included.
- **limitation cue**: An explicit limitation phrase in an answer ("one limitation", "we did not test", "future work"), from a lexicon. Hedging phrases ("I'm not sure", "maybe", "I think") are not limitation cues. A lexicon match, not an understanding of meaning.

## Measures

| Measure | Status | Needs | Languages | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `text.central_message.statements` | available | none | en | Sentences that match the thesis-cue lexicon, anywhere in the talk. |
| `text.central_message.in_opening` | available | none | en | Yes if the thesis sentence is in the opening. |
| `text.central_message.in_closing` | available | none | en | Yes if a sentence of the closing contains at least one key phrase. |

| `text.hook.present` | available | none | en | Yes if a hook is found (see the definition). |
| `text.support.items` | available | none | en | Supporting items, counted as (sentence, type) pairs. |
| `text.support.types` | available | none | en | Distinct supporting-material types found (0-4). |
| `text.language.undefined_terms` | available | none | en | Rare terms (word frequency below the rare-word threshold) used without a definition cue ("which means", "that is") in the same or the next sentence. |
| `text.language.mean_sentence_words` | available | none | en | Mean words per sentence. Missing when the punctuation is unreliable (ADR 0011): then only this condition is dropped and the language dimension rests on undefined terms. |
| `fillers.per_100_words` | available | none | en, ar | Hesitations per 100 words (speech/fillers.py). With base Whisper this is a lower bound and Arabic is low confidence (ADR 0012). |
| `pauses.mid_sentence_per_min` | available | none | en | Mid-sentence pauses (see the definition) per minute of speaking time (first speech onset to last offset, as rate.wpm), from the pause events of speech/pauses.py (rubric/derived.py). Missing when the punctuation is unreliable or for Arabic. |
| `vision.gaze.at_camera_share` | planned (P6) | camera | en, ar | Share of speaking time with gaze zone at_camera (ADR 0004); camera on only. |
| `timing.overrun_s` | available | session_limit | en, ar | Seconds spoken beyond the session's time limit (0 when within it). |
| `followup.relevance_min` | available | followups | en | Lowest question-answer similarity over the follow-up answers (see relevance). Not measured for Arabic until there are Arabic calibration pairs (ADR 0022). |
| `followup.latency_max_s` | available | followups | en, ar | Longest response latency over the follow-up answers (see response latency). Missing for an answer whose recording has no speech onset; then only this condition is dropped. |
| `followup.limitation_acknowledged` | available | followups | en | Yes if at least one follow-up answer contains a limitation cue. |
